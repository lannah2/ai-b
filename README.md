*Abstract*

History in Motion: Reimagining Football Heritage explores the significance of human-AI collaboration in the reinterpretation and preservation of cultural heritage through immersive digital media. Centred around Free Kicks at Football (1882), a historical football pamphlet held within the University of Glasgow’s Archives and Special Collections, the project transforms the artefact into an audiovisual experience combining AI-generated video, text-to-speech narration, and archival content interpretation. Focusing primarily on Benedict’s poem A “Lay-On” the Leather, the project utilises generative AI to interpret Victorian diction, construct historically accurate visual sequences, and recreate the atmosphere and emotional and societal significance of nineteenth-century football culture for contemporary audiences. This is achieved through the implementation of concepts such as digital nostalgia and synthetic memory.

Alongside its creative outcomes, the project critically evaluates the limitations of generative AI. Issues such as inconsistency, deviation from prompts, inaccurate historical details, and unnatural AI narration revealed the current technological constraints of text-to-video and text-to-speech software. The concept of AI hallucination is also explored through the creation of an entirely fictional AI-generated poem and accompanying video regarding the 1921 ban on women’s football. This project demonstrates how human-AI collaboration may reshape the future of cultural preservation and public historical engagement for a range of demographics.
